structure-first-ml

Structure-first machine learning for physical data  ·  Enes Öz  ·  2026

Premise

Most machine-learning pipelines coerce data into the shape the model expects: irregular observations are interpolated onto a regular grid, point clouds are voxelised, curved domains are flattened, and conserved quantities are left to be learned by accident. Every one of those steps discards structure that was present in the measurement and introduces artefacts that were not. This laboratory reverses the order of reasoning.

Method

Identify the mathematical object the data belongs to — a path, a point cloud, a section of a bundle, an orbit of a dynamical system — then choose a representation with the invariances that object actually has, and only then attach a learning algorithm. Where the representation carries a theorem, state the theorem and test whether its hypotheses hold on real data.

The commitment is methodological rather than stylistic, because it yields falsifiable claims. A representation backed by a theorem predicts where it should beat a baseline and, equally, where it should not. Both predictions are reported.

Tracks

T1 — Path signatures for irregularly sampled light curves closed: complementary on transients, useless on periodics

Astronomical photometry is a sampled path, not a vector: observations arrive at irregular times, in different bands, with gaps set by weather and scheduling. The signature of a path, $S(X)=\left(1,\int dX,\iint dX\otimes dX,\dots\right)$, is a reparameterisation-invariant feature map that consumes the raw $(t,\,\text{flux},\,\sigma,\,\text{band})$ stream with no interpolation at all. Standard in finance and healthcare, it has exactly one published astronomical application (radio-interferometric interference detection); light-curve classification was untouched.

On 2,375 spectroscopically classified ZTF transients in 8 classes, signatures lose to a 49-feature hand-crafted baseline (0.547 against 0.562 balanced accuracy) while using fourteen times as many features. But concatenated with that baseline they reach 0.577, and a matched-noise control settles what that means: 680 columns of noise cost 0.035, while 680 signature columns gain 0.015, under identical folds. The information is real and it is complementary. A separate synthetic control located the mechanism — ordering becomes visible at truncation depth 3, not depth 2 as predicted, because a path monotone in time encloses no signed area for the Lévy term to see.

A counter-test on 2,999 Gaia periodic variables closes the track symmetrically. There signatures lose six times harder (baseline gap 0.074 against ZTF's 0.012), the complementarity that made the ZTF result worth reporting vanishes entirely (−0.0000 against +0.0147), and — the sharpest form of it — a single catalogued period beats a 1,020-coefficient signature (0.773 against 0.741). Period is a statement about the clock, and the clock is exactly what reparameterisation invariance discards. The property that produces the win produces the loss.

T2 — Meteoroid stream discovery result: verified method, nothing new

2.15 million meteor orbits from the Global Meteor Network, clustered under several classical orbital dissimilarity criteria at once, where a candidate must survive all of them. The field's own 2025 methods review recommends exactly this and nobody has run it on this archive.

A first pass returned 3,727 apparently novel groups; an audit showed they were the sporadic background itself. The decisive fix was the null model, and it was diagnosed by measurement rather than argument: the standard permutation null scores 0.80 on separation — it predicts the same density at a real shower as in empty background, because the sporadic sources exist through element correlations that shuffling destroys. A sideband null built from real orbits at adjacent solar longitudes, with nodes rotated to the window's geometry, reaches 7.48. Swapping it cut unmatched structures from 498 to 56 with no loss of known-shower recovery, and turned the recurrence filter from a 4.5-point separation into a 40.7-point one.

Of 21 candidates surviving every filter, the 8 strongest were re-checked at loose tolerance against every IAU entry — and 8 of 8 are known showers, two within 2°. The remaining false positives came from the matching step, not the clustering. The method recovers 57% of the established catalogue and invents nothing: a clean negative on discovery, and a quantitative result on null models that stands on its own.

T3 — Where ordering lives in the signature result: prediction refuted at proof strength

The theory track, built to explain T1's numbers. Ordering information turns out to appear at truncation depth 3, not depth 2 as predicted, and the reason is structural: with a monotone time channel the level-2 Lévy area reduces to the area under the curve, which is identical whichever event came first. Since every time-augmented signature of a time series has a monotone time channel, this is a property of the standard construction rather than a quirk.

The follow-up prediction — that the lead-lag transform restores ordering to level 2 — is refuted exactly: on time-reversed pairs the level-2 tensors agree to 1.2e-15, because lead-lag's entire depth-≤2 content is quadratic variation, which is order-blind by construction. What does work is a causal, memory-carrying channel: a running maximum reaches at level 2 what the plain path only reaches at level 3. Every verification check was deliberately broken to confirm it could fail.

T4 — Plate topology: a century of sky as image structure closed: distinguishes places, not times

From time series to images, and a deliberate reuse: persistent homology was rejected earlier in this repository because degree-1 Vietoris–Rips on Gaia phase-space point clouds is intractable. On a pixel grid the natural construction is a cubical complex with a sublevel-set filtration — near-linear, four million cells in seconds. The same mathematics that was unusable on a point cloud is routine on an image.

The archive is the Harvard plate collection: 429,274 plates, 1880s–1990s, digitisation completed 2024, and a century-long time axis no modern survey has. Of twenty papers using it, eighteen extract point-source light curves and nobody treats a plate series as an $(x, y, \text{epoch})$ object.

Four experiments, no pipeline, and a clean negative. The premise survived three of them. Z-score normalisation opened field-to-field separation to 0.628 — and refuted the argument written in advance for the rank transform, which came last because it preserves the topology and destroys the metric the comparison uses. The fourth experiment is the one the track depended on: does a field containing GK Persei, which erupted in 1901 from magnitude 13 to 0.2, separate from its own earlier epochs? It does not — change signal −0.399 against a threshold of +0.314 fixed beforehand, and in the wrong direction. The diagnosis is dilution: whole-field diagrams are driven by which stars sit in the field, and one nova among thousands does not move them.
